# AccuFi M2 — decisions taken during implementation

Every ruling made while executing `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-20-accufi-m2-investments.md`
(investments: quantity and lump valuation, lots, FIFO/LIFO/highest-cost sales, prices, staleness).
The first nine are the plan's own "Calls made" (decisions the plan took where the spec was silent or
wrong, written before any code); the rest are the controller's rulings from the execution ledger
(`.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-20-accufi-m2-investments/progress.md`), in the order they were made.
Each records what was ruled, why, and what it costs if it turns out to be wrong.

The numbering is this file's own (R1 to R27). Where a later ruling amends an earlier one, both say so.

---

## The plan's nine calls (made before any code was written)

### R1: Remaining quantity is derived, not a column

What: the lot has no `quantityRemaining` column. Remaining = `quantity − Σ live consumptions`, where a lot is live while its buying transaction is not deleted and a consumption is live while its disposal transaction is not deleted. Task 4 amended spec §7.2 (now stated there in so many words).
Why: spec §9.4 forbids cached figures for the reason that one missed path corrupts them invisibly. Derived, deleting or restoring a sale needs no bookkeeping at all.
Cost if wrong: every read of a holding does a join and a sum instead of reading a column; if that is ever too slow, a cache would have to be kept right by every create, edit, delete and restore path.

### R2: The spec's partial-consumption example is inconsistent

What: the spec says "selling 8g" but its arithmetic consumes 13g (10 + 3). The plan tests the arithmetic as written: 13g → ₹70,000 + ₹22,200 = ₹92,200.
Why: the figures are the part a test can pin; the word "8g" is a typo in the example.
Cost if wrong: the allocation rule is right either way (it is FIFO by quantity); only the example's prose would have to be corrected.

### R3: Selling has its own "Sell / redeem" form; the generic form refuses what would drift the lots

What: a sale is entered on the investment's own Sell / redeem form. A transaction out of a QUANTITY or LUMP investment through the generic form is refused with a pointer to Sell, so lots can never drift from the ledger. Fund switches (`INVESTMENT → INVESTMENT` with a lot-mode end) and fees out of a lot-mode investment are refused in M2 ("sell, then buy"). CONTRIBUTION_ONLY investments keep every existing flow (EC-28).
Why: a sale has to choose lots, post a cost-basis leg and a gain or loss leg and keep the consumptions in step; the generic form has none of that, and letting it write the same legs would let the two disagree.
Cost if wrong: a user who switches funds or pays a fee out of a lot-mode holding has to sell and buy instead; the refusals can be lifted one flow at a time once the lots rules cover them.

### R4: A disposal posts as spec §7.5 and cannot be edited

What: a gain posts a cost-basis leg and a Realised Gain leg; a loss posts a proceeds leg and a Realised Loss leg; break-even posts one plain leg. A disposal is deleted and restored as a unit and cannot be edited in M2 ("delete and enter it again").
Why: an edit would have to re-allocate lots, re-post legs and re-check every later sale in one step; delete-and-re-enter reuses paths that are already guarded.
Cost if wrong: correcting a sale takes two actions instead of one; the amount the user sees on the Transactions screen is the same.

### R5: Cost basis of a partial lot, and current value

What: cost basis of a partial take is `round_half_up(costBasisRemaining × take ÷ remaining)`; taking the whole remainder takes `costBasisRemaining` exactly, so lots always sum to the true cost. Current value is `round_half_up(Σ open quantity × price)`, rounded once per node.
Why: rounding once at the end of a holding, and never leaving a paisa behind on a lot that is fully sold, keeps cost and value reconcilable to the paisa.
Cost if wrong: at most a paisa of difference on a partly sold lot against some other convention; the lots themselves still sum exactly to what was paid.

### R6: Valuation is "as of a day"

What: open quantity at day D counts lots acquired on or before D and consumptions disposed on or before D. A disposal may only consume lots acquired on or before its date. Net worth values at today. (The plan said the projected figure "reuses today's valuation"; R19 amends that.)
Why: it makes a back-dated or future-dated purchase or sale land in the right figure, and it is the only definition under which restore and delete of a sale are well defined.
Cost if wrong: back-dated entries would change figures that were already reported; the as-of reading is strictly more precise.

### R7: Existing investment data is "not valued" until it has a quantity

What: a lot-mode node whose live inflows lack a lot, or that has an outflow with no consumption, is not valued (reason "needs quantity") until those entries are put right. Nothing is guessed or backfilled. (R27 says how each kind of entry is put right; editing to add a quantity is only one of them.) The user's own "Nifty Index Fund" reads "not valued" until it has a quantity and a price.
Why: a quantity guessed from an amount would be a figure the user never entered, shown as if it were theirs (EC-23, EC-24).
Cost if wrong: one-time manual work per old investment; net worth reads "partial" and names the investment until it is done.

### R8: A price cannot be dated after today

What: a forecast is not a price, so a price dated after today is refused (`PRICE_AFTER_TODAY`). Any earlier date is accepted (EC-25). Re-entering a date overwrites it (EC-26).
Why: a future-dated price would value the holding today at a figure that is not yet true, and could outrank a real one.
Cost if wrong: a user who wants to record a planned price cannot; nothing is lost, the price can be entered on its day.

### R9: Staleness

What: the window is `LedgerNode.stalePriceDays ?? User.stalePriceDays` (default 30); a price is stale when `today − price.asOf > window`.
Why: a per-investment override is needed (a fund priced monthly versus gold priced daily) and a user default avoids setting it everywhere.
Cost if wrong: a wrong default would flag too many or too few prices as stale; the window is a single field per node or user.

## Rulings from the execution ledger

### R10: Models for the subagents

What: Sonnet for Tasks 1-3 and 8; Opus for Tasks 5-7 and 9-11; Sonnet reviewers; Opus for the final review.
Why: turn count against cost: the tasks that carry the hard concurrency and rounding rules get the stronger model.
Cost if wrong: slower or more expensive than it needed to be; no effect on the code.

### R11: Playwright is not part of the Task 1-8 gates

What: end-to-end tests are checked at Tasks 9-12; Tasks 1-8 run tsc, lint and Vitest. Tasks 7 and 8 were to run the full e2e once as the mitigation.
Why: the screens did not exist until Task 9, and a browser run on every domain task would slow the loop for no signal.
Cost if wrong: a regression in an existing e2e between Task 5 and Task 9 would have surfaced late (mitigated by the one full run planned at Tasks 7 and 8).

### R12: PriceSnapshot keeps ON DELETE RESTRICT; deleting a node deletes its prices (Task 4)

What: the foreign key from `PriceSnapshot` to `LedgerNode` stays RESTRICT. Instead `removeLedgerNode` deletes the node's snapshots when the node has no transactions, inside one transaction (now `deletePricesOfNode` called from `node-service.ts`).
Why: a cascade would silently destroy hand-entered prices; an explicit delete in the node-removal transaction does it only where deletion is already allowed.
Cost if wrong: a snapshot-bearing investment with no transactions could not be deleted until Task 5 landed the fix (it did).

### R13: A corrective migration, not a database reset (Task 4)

What: when the `LotConsumption` basis CHECK had to be relaxed from `> 0` to `>= 0` (a zero-paisa partial take is legitimate), the fix is a new migration (`20261020190000`, drop and re-add the constraint, renamed `..._nonnegative`), applied with `migrate deploy`. A `migrate reset` of the test database was refused by the tooling and was not circumvented.
Why: a reset needs the user's own consent; a forward migration needs nothing and carries no data risk.
Cost if wrong: two migrations for one feature, so the history is slightly noisier.

### R14: A lump investment is bought once (Task 5)

What: a LUMP investment accepts one live purchase only. A second is refused: "A lump investment is bought once; add another investment instead". Enforced on record, edit and restore, with the rules in `src/server/services/lot-rules.ts`.
Why: spec §7.1 says a lump is a single lot of quantity 1; with several 1-unit lots a lump price would value the holding once per lot.
Cost if wrong: add-ons to a lump (improvements, stamp duty on a plot) have to be a separate investment; see "What M2 does not do".

### R15: Spaces around a quantity are trimmed (Task 5)

What: `" 5 "` is trimmed and accepted by `parseQuantity`, like every other text field. The plan's review-focus bullet that said otherwise over-stated it.
Why: consistency with how every other field in the app treats surrounding whitespace.
Cost if wrong: none; a user who types a stray space is not told off.

### R16: Edit/delete versus sale race parked, then unparked (Task 5, then Task 6)

What: in Task 5 the race between editing or deleting a purchase and a concurrent sale of the same lot (one user, two tabs) was parked: row locking is the remedy, the final review would see it. In Task 6 the controller reversed this once `lockHoldings` existed: purchase edit and delete take the investment's lock (at the top of `assertPurchaseRemovable` and before the lot read in `applyLotEdit`), with a mutation-checked test, and two in-transaction reads got a unit-of-work argument.
Why: the lock was a one-line call by then, and the race could over-consume or orphan a lot.
Cost if wrong: slightly more lock contention on one investment row, and two reads that must stay on the transaction's own connection.

### R17: A sale whose cost basis rounds to nothing is refused (Task 6)

What: a sale whose total cost basis rounds to 0 paise is refused (`QUANTITY_TOO_SMALL`).
Why: the sale's cost-basis consumption is posted as a leg, and a ₹0 leg cannot be posted; accepted as it stands rather than allowing a zero leg.
Cost if wrong: a dust lot (units left, ₹0 of remaining cost) cannot be sold through Sell; a later product decision (see "What M2 does not do").

### R18: Two races left parked at Task 6

What: (1) the "lump bought once" check on record and restore of a purchase runs without the investment lock, so two simultaneous first purchases of one lump could both pass (same-key double submits are already idempotent); one `lockHoldings` call each would close it. (2) `removeTransaction` chooses which node to lock from a row it read before the lock, so an uncommitted edit that moves a purchase between nodes could race a delete; a version or `toNodeId` guard on `softDeleteTransaction` is the remedy. Also noted: an M1 ungroup versus delete of the same group can deadlock on row locks (one request errors, no money moves), and no test asserts that ordinary rows take no node lock.
Why: parked so the final review sees them, not fixed in the task; each needs a deliberate multi-tab sequence from one user and has a known small remedy (a `lockHoldings` call, or a version or `toNodeId` guard).
Cost if wrong: two live lots in one lump, or a live consumption on a deleted purchase, in a rare two-tab case. Listed again under "Known and parked". (1) was closed for restore in Task 6 round 2 and for record by R25.

### R19: The projected figure values quantity as of the projection date, at today's price (Task 7; amends R6)

What: the projected net worth values each lot-mode node's quantity as of the projection date, priced at the latest price on or before today (today's price, not today's figure). Valued investments carry `{asOf, stale}` to the dashboard tile, the ledger row and a note on the portfolio card, shown as "as of <date> · Stale".
Why: spec §7.3 and EC-24 bind, and reusing today's figure literally would be wrong by the whole amount of any future-dated lot-mode purchase or sale.
Cost if wrong: a slightly larger Task 7 diff and two screens that carry the price's day.

### R20: Task 9's e2e sets prices through the seed helper (Task 9)

What: the investments e2e puts prices in with the `price` seed helper rather than the price screen. The price-screen scenarios live in Task 10's e2e.
Why: the price screen was Task 10's, so it did not exist when Task 9's tests were written.
Cost if wrong: "enter a price in the UI, then see the card revalue" was exercised only once Task 10 landed.

### R21: Purchases through the form are deferred to Task 11's e2e (Task 9)

What: the UI purchase step of Task 9's e2e (buying 10 g and 5 g on the Transactions form) was not possible until Task 11 built the Quantity field; Task 9 used the `buy` seed. Task 11's e2e extends the flow with purchases made through the form (quantity and unit price).
Why: there is no way to enter a quantity on the form before Task 11.
Cost if wrong: purchase-through-UI was first exercised at Task 11.

### R22: "Split from several sources" is the correct wording for a gain sale (Task 9)

What: Transactions shows "Split from several sources" for a gain sale (the gain leg comes from Realised Gain, the cost-basis leg from the investment). The brief's "Split from Gold" was wrong; the e2e pins the real text.
Why: the display is correct (the legs really do come from more than one source); the brief's wording was not.
Cost if wrong: none to the data; only the label, which can be reworded.

### R23: Deleting a price offers Undo instead of a confirmation (Task 10)

What: deleting a price asks nothing; the message that follows offers Undo, which saves the same price for the same day again.
Why: safer than no safeguard and cheaper than a confirmation dialogue on a routine correction.
Cost if wrong: Undo overwrites a price entered for that day in the meantime.

### R24: A lump's price field is "Price of the whole (₹)" (Task 10)

What: for a LUMP investment the price field is labelled "Price of the whole (₹)" with the hint "What the whole of it is worth.", not "Price per {unit} (₹)".
Why: a lump has no unit, so a "per" label would be wrong.
Cost if wrong: the label differs from the brief's text; nothing else changes.

### R25: The first purchase of a lump takes the investment's lock (final review; amends R14 and R18)

What: `recordTransaction` takes `lockLotHoldings(userId, [to], uow)` at the top of its write, before the row is inserted, so the "lump bought once" check (R14) runs under the investment's lock on record as it already did on restore. Two first purchases of one lump sent at once from two tabs with different request keys now record one and refuse the other (`LUMP_ALREADY_BOUGHT`); a double submit with one key still returns the first result. A purchase into a `QUANTITY` investment takes the same lock (one row lock; it serialises only purchases of that one investment). The order rule holds: node locks before row writes, in id order. Pinned by a 20-round race test in `tests/server/disposal.test.ts` that fails with the lock removed.
Why: without it both purchases passed the check and committed, and the lump was valued twice (two units at the whole price).
Cost if wrong: two simultaneous purchases of the same investment wait for each other for the length of one write.

### R26: Every screen shows an investment held by lots the same way, and a kind's total says what it leaves out (final review)

What: the Transactions screen's Balances read their figures from the ledger's own calculation (`loadValuationStates` with `buildNodeOverview`): an investment held by lots shows its value today with the price's day and a Stale label, or "Not valued" and the reason, as on the ledger and the dashboard; one valued by what was put in shows that money. `KindSummary` and `DashboardKindTotal` carry `unvaluedCount` and `archivedUnvaluedCount`; the Investments total on the ledger and on the dashboard says "excludes N not valued" beside it, in an element of its own, and the line about archived nodes never calls an unvalued archived investment included ("1 archived, not listed; it is not valued, so not counted."). The holdings split bar under net worth is left as it is: it is aria-hidden, draws only the valued figures the net worth card already calls partial, and an unvalued investment has no figure to draw.
Why: EC-23 (a missing value is never zero or cost) and one figure per node across screens. Before this, the Transactions screen showed gold at its remaining cost while the ledger showed its value, and a total of the valued investments read as the total of all of them.
Cost if wrong: one more valuation read per Transactions page load.

### R27: How each kind of legacy entry is put right, said per kind (final review; amends R7)

What: `findLotGaps` reports three kinds of gap: a purchase on its own with no lot, a purchase inside a split with no lot, and a withdrawal with no consumption. The holding carries them (`HoldingView.quantityGaps`), and the investment card and the price form say how to put each right: add the quantity (for a lump, open the purchase and save it, which records it as the single holding: the edit form sends a quantity of 1 for a lot-less purchase of a lump); take a split leg out of its split first; delete a withdrawal and enter it again with Sell. While a node needs a quantity its card's Held and Cost basis read "Not known" and it offers no Sell (the lots alone may overstate what is held). Also: the Sell form says which purchases a sale takes first by the investment's lot method (oldest, newest or dearest), and the portfolio card no longer calls a fully sold holding valued at a stale price.
Why: the old copy told the user to edit the entries to add a quantity, which the product could not do for a lump, a withdrawal or a split leg: a dead end.
Cost if wrong: the copy has three variants to keep true as the rules change.

## Known and parked

Not rulings: things the work found and chose not to fix. None moves money in a single-user, single-tab session.

Races (each needs one user in two tabs):

- `removeTransaction` picks the node to lock from a row read before the lock; an uncommitted edit that moves a purchase between nodes could race a delete. Lives in `src/server/services/transaction-service.ts`. Remedy: a version or `toNodeId` guard on `softDeleteTransaction`.
- M1: ungroup versus delete of the same group can deadlock on row locks (one request errors, no money impact). Lives in the group paths of `transaction-service.ts` and `transaction-repository.ts`.
- No test asserts that ordinary (non-lot) rows take no node lock. Lives in `tests/server/disposal.test.ts` and the lock tests.

Deferred minors, by where they live:

- `src/domain/quantity.ts`, `valuation.ts`: `valueOf`, `unitPriceOf`, `quantityFor` throw `RangeError` on negative or zero inputs; callers compute signed gains by subtraction. Round-trip property tests use an unseeded `Math.random`, a few edge cases are untested ("007", "1.5.5", a negative `formatQuantity`), and there is no test for a held, priced lot with zero basis, nor for EC-25 through `valueHolding` (only at `latestPrice`). `gainBasisPoints` rounds half away from zero and is null when not valued or cost is 0.
- `src/domain/lots.ts`: the allocator's property test is unseeded (`Math.random`) with a weak oracle for ordering; the enum-parity header comment is stale; `openLots` silently drops remaining ≤ 0 (add a comment).
- `src/server/repositories/lot-repository.ts`, `src/server/services/investment-service.ts`: `findLotGaps` treats every DISPOSAL-group outflow as covered (stricter would be "any leg has a consumption"); the `lot:{is:null}` filter lacks `userId` (harmless); `removeTransaction` and `assertNotASale` read outside the unit of work; a 1-paisa rounding drift can refuse a restore with a message that reads as if the user edited the purchase; the node and destination are read before the lock in the sale path.
- `src/server/services/investment-service.ts`: 540 lines (the read half could move to a holding service); `ANY_WINDOW = 0` is a dummy argument; INVESTMENT nodes are read twice per load; four queries per lot-mode investment per dashboard, ledger or Transactions load. `lotHoldingView` and `valuationStatesFrom` value a holding by two separate code paths (the portfolio's staleness guard had drifted from net worth's before the final review): a merge would remove the drift risk.
- Task 7's server tests (`tests/server/valuation-service.test.ts` and its neighbours): fixed 2026 dates are measured against the real today.
- `src/app/transactions/*` (pre-existing M1, seen by M2): the dashboard tile link puts a node id in its URL (spec §9.5 says ids stay out of URLs).
- `src/app/investments/*`: no test for "valued but cost 0 → null percent" and none for a hero holding only fully sold investments (₹0.00 and ₹0.00 as real zeros).
- `src/app/investments/prices/*`: the `removed` set is never cleared (transient wrong history after several writes); toasts do not name the investment; the NEEDS_QUANTITY e2e check is partly vacuous; "beside Date" is asserted only as text; two sleeps in the guard e2e; chatty live region; disabled Save or Delete drops focus on failure; the "Record prices" header link is not clicked in an e2e.
- `src/app/transactions/transaction-form.tsx` (over 800 lines, optional `PurchaseFields` split) and its e2e: "A single holding" is not in To's `aria-describedby`; the optional-count e2e is brittle.
- `tests/server/disposal.test.ts`: `restoreGate.afterFindRestorable` could leak into the next test if a test fails before reaching `findRestorable`.
- `src/server/action-support.ts` and `tests/server/investment-actions.test.ts`: no test that an unexpected service error is rethrown; one long comment line.
- Three weak assertions in the Task 5 tests (schema "is optional" is vacuous; "refuses amount below sold" does not assert the lot; "sold in full" does not assert the lot unchanged).
- `src/server/repositories/lot-repository.ts`, `price-repository.ts`: the ownership of a lot, consumption or price row by the node's user is enforced in the repository only (no database constraint ties the two); no negative-quantity consumption test. The isolation e2e (Task 12) now covers it from a browser.

## What M2 does not do

By design, and stated in the plan; each is a refusal or an absence, not a bug.

- Fund switches: `INVESTMENT → INVESTMENT` with a QUANTITY or LUMP end is refused ("sell, then buy"). R3.
- Fees out of a lot-mode investment: refused for the same reason; CONTRIBUTION_ONLY investments keep theirs. R3.
- Editing a sale: a disposal is deleted and entered again. R4.
- `source = FEED` prices: the column and the enum are a seam only; every price is entered by hand (`MANUAL`).
- Lump add-ons: a LUMP investment is bought once, so improvements or stamp duty on a plot go on a separate investment. R14.
- Zero-cost dust lots: a sale whose cost basis rounds to 0 paise is refused, so such a lot cannot be sold through Sell. R17.
- The dashboard link that carries a node id in its URL is from M1 and is left as it is; M2 adds no new URL with an id (the investments and prices screens select by control).
